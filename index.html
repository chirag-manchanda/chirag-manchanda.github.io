About

I’m an environmental engineer and postdoctoral scholar in the ECHO Lab at Stanford’s Doerr School of Sustainability, working with Marshall Burke.

At Stanford, I’m extending this work to wildfire emissions and smoke exposure, asking how prescribed burning can be planned to reduce future wildfire impacts while limiting harm to nearby and downwind communities.

I earned my Ph.D. in Environmental Engineering at UC Berkeley in 2026, advised by Joshua Apte and Robert Harley. My dissertation examined urban pollution, from reconstructing concentrations at city-block scales to estimating emissions and evaluating control strategies.

Before Berkeley, I studied particulate matter composition and sources at IIT Delhi. I’m interested in how environmental observations can inform decisions about cities and natural resources, particularly when the benefits and burdens fall unevenly across communities.

Research directions

I develop methods to study environmental processes from incomplete observations and evaluate how different interventions could change their outcomes.

01

Environmental observations and models

I combine measurements with atmospheric models to estimate conditions that we cannot observe directly, accounting for differences in resolution and uncertainty. Current work focuses on separating wildfire smoke from background pollution, with an interest in incorporating satellite and aircraft observations.

02

Emissions and urban systems

I use Bayesian inversion to estimate emissions from observed concentrations and examine contributions from roads and facilities. I’m exploring faster transport models and differentiable emulators to make repeated inversions practical and test how changes in urban activity could affect exposure.

03

Environmental policy and equity

I use optimization to compare strategies for meeting environmental targets and examine how their benefits are distributed across communities. I’m interested in policies that address air quality and climate together, including how households and firms respond and how those responses affect outcomes. For prescribed burning, I’m interested in where and when to burn, weighing immediate smoke exposure against potential reductions in future wildfire impacts and considering who bears those risks.
